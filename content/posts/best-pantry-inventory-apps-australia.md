A pantry inventory app almost always starts the same way: type it in, or scan a barcode, one item at a time. Pantry starts somewhere else entirely — a photographed receipt from Coles, Woolworths or Aldi, with a whole trolley becoming a tracked list in one pass. Before getting into why that matters, here is what the established options in this category offer, based on their own app pages and store listings.

## NoWaste

[NoWaste](https://www.nowasteapp.com/) organises food into separate freezer, fridge and pantry lists, with barcode scanning and photo recognition to add items. It includes an AI assistant for voice or text commands, and its free tier covers six lists and 500 items. NoWaste runs on both iOS and Android, and started as a solo-developer project rather than a larger company’s product — a focused, no-frills tracker rather than a broader “kitchen platform”.

## KitchenPal

[KitchenPal](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/kitchen-pal-food-pantry-app/id1084982489) goes wider than pure inventory: shared grocery lists, barcode scanning, meal planning and recipe discovery sit alongside pantry tracking, with family sharing built in. It is rated 4.4 out of 5 across 95 App Store ratings. Pricing runs to Premium at $5.99 a month (or $8.99 for three months, $22.99 a year), with lifetime options at $49.99 for one person and $59.99 for a family pack. For a household that wants meal planning and a shopping list bundled in with tracking, KitchenPal covers more ground than a dedicated tracker.

## Fridgely

[Fridgely](https://fridgelyapp.com/) is iOS-only and leans specifically into expiry alerts: barcode scanning, shared access for family or flatmates, separate spaces for fridge, pantry and freezer, and recipe suggestions based on what is already logged. It has been available since 2015, making it one of the longer-running dedicated trackers in the category.

## Pantry Check

Pantry Check takes the narrowest approach of the four: fast manual entry and a clear view of what is coming up on its use-by date, with no barcode scanning and none of the extras the others carry. If the goal is the plainest possible interface for tracking dates by hand, it is built for exactly that.

## Where Pantry starts differently

Every app above still needs something typed or scanned in one at a time — a barcode, a name, a use-by date keyed in by hand. Pantry starts from the receipt instead. Photograph the docket from the shop and the whole trolley becomes a list at once, with an estimated expiry date attached to each item from the same shelf-life research behind [our storage guide](/resources/how-long-food-lasts).

Sharing is the other place Pantry is built differently rather than added on afterwards. Several of the apps above support a shared list; Pantry Pro is designed around one from the start, a single subscription covering up to six people so nobody in the house is paying twice to see the same fridge. The [household sharing post](/resources/sharehouse-fridge-rule) goes into why that combination matters more than the tracking feature on its own.
