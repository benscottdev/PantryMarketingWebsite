Wednesday takeaway rarely starts with a craving. It starts with an open fridge at 6pm, no obvious dinner in it, and a hand reaching for the phone instead of a pan — not because cooking would have taken longer, but because deciding what to cook felt like the harder problem in that moment.

## The real cost is not the takeaway itself

An occasional takeaway is not a failure of anything. The real cost shows up alongside it: the chicken that was meant to be Wednesday’s dinner is still in the fridge on Thursday, one day closer to [its three-day window](/resources/chicken-in-the-fridge-three-days) closing, because delivery took its slot. Takeaway does not just cost the price of the order. It costs whatever ingredient was waiting to become that meal, pushed one day nearer the bin.

## What the Sunday hour needs to cover

Not a week of pre-cooked meals stacked in containers — that is a bigger commitment than most households keep up past a few weeks. The hour that stops Wednesday takeaway is smaller and more specific: open the fridge, see what is already in it, and assign each thing to a night before the week starts making its own competing plans.

Three things, in order:
- **Check what has the shortest window.** [Chicken](/resources/chicken-in-the-fridge-three-days), [mince](/resources/mince-going-off-tonight) and anything leafy go on the calendar first, because they have the least flexibility about which night works.
- **Assign a night, not a full menu.** “Tuesday: the chicken” is enough. The specific recipe can wait until Tuesday afternoon — the commitment that Tuesday is chicken night is what matters.
- **Leave one night open, on purpose.** A week with zero flexibility is a week where one changed plan cascades into everything else shifting. One deliberately unplanned night absorbs that without derailing the rest.

## Why this beats a full meal-prep session

Batch-cooking five dinners on Sunday is a real strategy, and it works for some households. It is also a bigger weekly commitment, and [batch-cooked food still only keeps three days in the fridge](/resources/how-long-do-leftovers-keep) before the back half needs the freezer anyway. The lighter version — assigning existing ingredients to nights, not pre-cooking them — takes a fraction of the time and solves the real problem. Not “what should I cook”, asked cold at 6pm, but “what was I already planning to cook”, answered in advance while there is still time to think about it.

## Where the hour pays off

The Sunday hour does not pay off on Sunday. It pays off on Wednesday at 6pm, when the fridge opens and there is already an answer — chicken, tonight, that was the plan — instead of a decision made from scratch while hungry and tired, which is exactly the condition under which takeaway wins by default.

## Where Pantry fits into the hour

The Sunday check is faster with a list in front of you than with a fridge you have to stand in front of and inventory by eye. Pantry’s list is built from [scanned receipts](/#how), not typed in from memory, so it already holds everything currently in the house — and how many days each item realistically has left. “What should we plan around” stops being a guess standing at the fridge and becomes a five-minute glance at a list that already knows the answer, before the week even starts.
