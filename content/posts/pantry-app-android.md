No, Pantry is not on Android, and it will not be at launch. Pantry is an iOS app for Australian households, per its own FAQ, and the first release on [the roadmap](/changelog) is the launch for iPhone. The published roadmap does not list an Android version, so this page will not invent a date for one. If you are on Android, the waitlist still takes your email, and it is where Pantry’s launch news goes first.

## Pantry on Android: the short answer

Pantry runs on iPhone. There is no Android build and no Android date on the roadmap. If that changes, the roadmap on [the changelog page](/changelog) is where it will show, because that page is the one place Pantry publishes what is coming and in what order.

## Why iPhone first

The roadmap is short, and every card on it is about getting one thing right. The 1.0 card is the iPhone launch. The Pro card, marked “At launch”, is the shared household. The card after that, marked “After launch”, is better item names off messy Woolies and Coles slips and meals that lean harder on what is about to expire. That is a list about reading Australian receipts well, not about reaching more platforms.

The reading is the hard part. Supermarket dockets are abbreviated, a thermal slip left in a warm car fades by Friday, and Pantry turns each line into a named item with a dated window. [How Pantry reads a faded receipt](/resources/how-pantry-reads-a-receipt) shows how much of the product lives in that one step, and [the supermarket receipts post](/resources/does-pantry-read-coles-woolworths-aldi-receipts) covers the three big chains.

## What an Android phone can do now

Three things work in any browser, on any phone.

- **Join the waitlist.** The form asks for an email address and nothing about your phone. It sends beta news, launch news and founding pricing.
- **Use the shelf-life table.** [How long food lasts](/resources/how-long-food-lasts) is the same CSIRO-grounded data Pantry’s expiry windows are built on, laid out as a table you can check from the fridge door.
- **Run the calculator.** [The food waste calculator](/calculator) estimates what your household bins each year, from how often you shop, who you feed and what you spend.

## Mixed households: one iPhone, one Android

This is where iPhone first matters most. Pro puts up to six people on one shared pantry, on one subscription, and everyone else in the house joins free by invite. Pantry is an iPhone app, so a member needs an iPhone to see that list.

In a house with one iPhone and one Android phone, the iPhone owner can still scan every receipt, get the morning digest and cook from the meal suggestions. The Android owner cannot see the list from the shop, and that is the exact gap [six phones, one pantry](/resources/six-phones-one-pantry) is about closing: whoever bought the milk knows how old it is, and nobody else does. Free covers one person, so a single iPhone in the house loses nothing by starting there. [The Free vs Pro post](/resources/pantry-free-vs-pro) sets out where the line between the plans falls.

## When does Pantry launch on iPhone?

The roadmap marks the iPhone launch as “Soon”, a window rather than a date, and the page says its windows move. [When does Pantry launch](/resources/when-does-pantry-launch) covers what arrives on launch day and what comes after. Pro is $4.99 a month or $49.99 a year, billed by Apple with seven days free, and waitlist members get founding pricing.

## Australia first, iPhone first

Both choices come from the same place. Pantry’s FAQ says the receipt models are trained on Australian receipt data in English, so Australia is where they work properly, and other countries come later. The app is built for one platform and one country’s supermarkets before it is built for more. That is a narrow promise, and it is the one Pantry can keep.

## Get on the list either way

Join [the waitlist](/#waitlist), whichever phone is in your pocket. An iPhone gets you onto the first release and founding pricing. An Android phone gets you the launch news, the shelf-life table and the calculator, and a seat on the list if the roadmap ever adds the platform. Pantry starts with a photographed receipt and a dated list on every iPhone in the house.
