The way to track food waste at home is to count it before it happens, not after. A bin diary fails for a reason the Fight Food Waste CRC has measured: Australian households under-report their own food waste by more than half, because it happens in amounts too small to notice, half a bag of spinach here and the end of a carton of cream there. Pantry tracks the other side of the same number. Its morning digest says what is turning this week and what it is worth, in dollars off the receipt, before any of it reaches the bin.

## Why the bin diary undercounts

Ask a household what it wastes and the answer is wrong in a consistent direction. The [Rabobank Financial Health Barometer](https://www.rabobank.com.au/foodwaste) found Australians self-report wasting about 11.1% of their groceries. The Fight Food Waste CRC’s measured figure for the same households is [$2,500 and 265 kilograms a year](/resources/household-food-waste-by-the-numbers), more than twice what they think. It is not that people lie about it. It is that nobody remembers the last quarter of the loaf, the three rashers of bacon, the yoghurt a week past its date. A diary that depends on noticing the waste inherits the same blind spot as the memory it is meant to replace.

## Count what is turning, not what was thrown out

Pantry does not ask what went in the bin. It knows what came home, because the receipt said so, and it knows when each item’s window closes, from [the shelf-life data behind every countdown](/resources/where-pantrys-expiry-dates-come-from). That is enough to count the waste before it is waste. The morning digest, in Pantry’s own example of it, reads: “3 things expire this week. $37.47 potentially going to waste.” Three items, priced from the receipt they came off, inside the last days of their windows. Every plan gets that digest, every morning, at 7:30am.

The number is small on purpose. A yearly total is something to feel bad about in January. Thirty-seven dollars on a Tuesday is something to cook.

## A number you can still act on

That is the difference between tracking waste and preventing it. A diary records a loss after the fact. The digest records a loss that has not happened yet, and on Pro, [the 5:30pm nudge](/resources/why-the-nudge-lands-at-530) follows it up with a meal for the item nearest the edge, while the shops are open and dinner is undecided. The bacon that was going to be Friday’s waste is Thursday’s carbonara, and the week’s figure drops without anyone writing anything down.

## What a year of Tuesdays adds up to

Household waste of $2,500 a year works out to [about $48 a week](/resources/what-food-waste-costs-per-week), and it arrives in exactly the small, unnoticed amounts a diary misses. The digest is built to make that $48 visible seven mornings at a time, which is the only scale at which a household can change it. Nobody acts on a yearly total. People act on the mince.

## The January version

January is when households resolve to waste less, and resolutions fail for the same reason bin diaries do: they run on attention, and attention is the thing a Tuesday evening has least of. The tracking that works is the kind that does not need anyone to remember it. Photograph the receipt on the way in from the car. Read one line at 7:30am. When Pantry ships, that is the whole system, and it counts the waste a diary was never going to see.
