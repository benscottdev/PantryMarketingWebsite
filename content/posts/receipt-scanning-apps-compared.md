Pantry starts from a photographed receipt, not a barcode scanned one item at a time. Point the camera at a Coles, Woolworths or Aldi docket and the whole shop becomes a dated list in one pass. A handful of other apps have started building toward the same idea. Here is what Fango, Eatvora and Alydo say about their own receipt scanning, and where Pantry sits next to them.

## Fango

[Fango](https://fango.fi/en/blog/pantry-check-app-alternatives/) photographs a receipt and reads every item at once, estimating an expiry date for each from what it is, per its own listing. It covers receipts from around 34 countries in 8 languages, reads a PDF receipt as well as a photo, and keeps data on-device rather than in the cloud, per its own listing. It is available on both Google Play and the App Store. That breadth is the point: Fango is built to handle receipt formats from dozens of countries, not tuned to any single one.

## Eatvora

Eatvora positions itself as a full kitchen inventory tool rather than a dedicated scanner — AI-generated recipes from what is on hand, plus a “pantry health score” for the household, sit alongside expiry tracking. Its free tier includes those AI features at no cost, with a paid tier for unlimited use. It plays the same role here that KitchenPal plays elsewhere in this category: a kitchen-management product first, an inventory tracker second.

## Alydo

[Alydo](https://alydo.app/) is the one built around the same local-receipt problem Pantry is solving for: Australian supermarket formats, and the abbreviated product names that show up on thermal-printed dockets from Coles, Woolworths, Aldi, IGA and Costco — a line like “WW FF MILK 2L” reading as Woolworths full-cream milk. Each scan captures individual items with prices and categorises them automatically.

## Where Pantry differs

Broad, multi-country apps like Fango solve a different problem: handling enormous variation in receipt formats across dozens of markets at once. Pantry solves a narrower one: one country to get right. Coles and Woolworths abbreviations, Aldi’s shorter dockets, IGA and Costco’s own formats — all read the way an Australian shopper already reads them.

Getting items into the list quickly is only half the job, though. None of the three above solves the other half: a shared household seeing the same list at the same time. That is where Pantry Pro’s household sharing comes in — up to six people on one subscription, built to sit directly on top of the receipt-scan entry point rather than bolted on afterwards. Scanning fast and seeing the same list are the same feature here, not two separate ones. The [household sharing post](/resources/sharehouse-fridge-rule) covers why that combination matters more than either half alone.

## Where Pantry stands right now

Pantry has not launched. It is on the waitlist, not the App Store, so none of this is a head-to-head test against Fango, Eatvora or Alydo. What is written above reflects how each app presents itself on its own site or listing, not an independent benchmark of accuracy or speed. When Pantry ships, the comparison that matters is the one you run yourself — a docket from your own kitchen, scanned on your own phone, against whichever of these you have used before.
