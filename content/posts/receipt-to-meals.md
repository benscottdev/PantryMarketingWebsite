Pantry, an iOS app for Australian households, turns a receipt into dinner in three moves, and none of them is a recipe search. The receipt scan puts every item in the house on one dated list. The meal suggestion is written from that list alone, so every ingredient is something you already own. And it starts from whatever is turning first, because, as Pantry’s own meals section puts it, mince with two days left beats yoghurt with a week. That is the whole answer to “what can I make with what is in my fridge”: the app already knows what is in there, because it read the docket.

## Every ingredient is already in the house

A recipe app answers the question in the wrong order. It picks a dish, then hands you a list of what to buy for it, and the mince that prompted the search is still in the fridge on Thursday. Pantry starts from the list the receipt built. A suggestion is assembled from items with a green dot next to them, meaning in the house, not on another shopping list, and anything added by hand counts the same as anything scanned. If the suggestion is lettuce wraps, it is because the turkey mince, the lettuce and the sauce are on the list, not because lettuce wraps are popular this week.

## Whatever is turning first

The list carries a window for every item, [taken at the cautious end of CSIRO’s published guidance](/resources/where-pantrys-expiry-dates-come-from), and the suggestion leans on the shortest one. Tracking expiry is only useful if it changes what you cook tonight, and this is the step where it does: the mince with two days left is the ingredient the dinner is built around, and the block of cheddar with a month on it waits its turn. The roadmap’s first post-launch item is to make that lean harder still, so the meals follow what is about to expire even more closely than they do at launch.

## Costed from the receipt

Each suggestion is priced from the receipts you scanned, so the dinner comes with a number attached, and the number is what it already cost. That changes the 5:30pm decision in a way a recipe never does. Takeaway is a new spend. The suggestion is a spend already made, sitting in the fridge, and a dinner that is already paid for is a harder thing to order pizza past.

## Two a week on Free, unlimited on Pro

The free plan includes two meal generations a week, built from whatever a receipt has already logged. [Pro](/resources/pantry-free-vs-pro) makes them unlimited and adds the delivery mechanism that matters most: the afternoon nudge, which arrives at 5:30pm naming the ingredient and the meal, on every phone in the household, while acting on it is still possible. [The timing is deliberate](/resources/why-the-nudge-lands-at-530). A suggestion at 11am reaches someone at work. The same suggestion at 5:30pm reaches someone deciding.

## Where the list can be wrong, and what happens then

A suggestion is only as good as the list it is written from, and the list comes off a supermarket receipt, which is a poor document: faded, abbreviated, and inconsistent between chains. Some lines come back wrong. Pantry does not quote an accuracy percentage for the scan, because there is no test set behind one that it could show you the workings for, and a number nobody can check is not worth printing. What it does instead is show you the list after every scan. You confirm it, fixing a wrong line takes two taps, and Pantry remembers the correction so the same receipt shorthand reads correctly next time. [How the receipt gets read](/resources/how-pantry-reads-a-receipt) has the detail. The thirty seconds of checking on shopping day is what makes Thursday’s suggestion worth trusting.

## Where this leaves the 5:30pm argument

“What is for dinner” is the question this whole site keeps returning to, because it is the moment the mince either becomes bolognese or becomes bin contents. A receipt that turns into a dated list, and a list that turns into a costed suggestion built from what is about to turn, answers that question before it is asked. When Pantry ships, that is the loop the free plan starts with: scan the shop, and let the shop suggest dinner.
