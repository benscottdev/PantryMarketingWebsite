Cooked leftovers keep three days in the fridge, two for cooked rice and pasta, per the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/consumer/special-care-foods/leftovers). That is the window. A container that went in on Sunday is dinner by Wednesday or it is the bin, and “we’ll have it tomorrow” is how most of them miss. A container on its last day does not need reheating so much as a second dinner built around it. Five, each from pantry basics plus whatever is in the container.

## 1. Fried rice, if the rice is on day two

Rice is the short clock in the fridge — two days, not three — so leftover rice is tonight’s dinner, not tomorrow’s. Rice fried hard in a little oil, an egg scrambled through it, whatever cooked meat is in another container chopped in, and the vegetables going soft in the crisper. Ten minutes, one pan, and it solves two problems if the [spinach is wilting](/resources/wilting-spinach-five-dinners) at the same time.

## 2. A frittata from the roast

Roast vegetables, cooked potatoes, the last of a roast chicken, the end of a cheese block: anything cooked and solid goes into a frittata. Beaten eggs hold it together, a pan on the stove starts it and the oven or grill finishes the top. [Eggs](/resources/how-long-do-eggs-last) are almost always in the house, and it eats as well cold the next day as it does hot, which buys the leftovers one more meal without a third reheat.

## 3. A toastie or a quesadilla

Anything saucy folds into bread. Bolognese, chilli, curry, pulled or roast meat with a spoon of whatever sauce it came with, and cheese to hold it together, pressed in a pan or a toastie maker. The fastest of the five, and the one for the night when the container is a single serve that will not stretch to a plate on its own.

## 4. Soup, from the carcass or the sauce

A roast chicken carcass and the last of the vegetables, covered with stock and simmered while the rest of the evening happens. Or the half-container of bolognese that becomes a minestrone with a tin of beans, a tin of tomatoes and a handful of pasta. Soup is forgiving about quantities, which is what makes it the right shape for leftovers that do not add up to a meal on their own.

## 5. The bake

Leftover pasta or a cooked grain, leftover vegetables, a sauce from the cupboard — a tin of tomatoes, or a white sauce from butter, flour and milk — and cheese over the top, into the oven until it browns. Longer than the others, but the active time is short, and it is the one to reach for when the leftovers have to be used tonight and nobody wants to eat for another hour. It holds and reheats better than anything else on this list.

## Reheat it properly

Whichever of the five it becomes, the NSW Food Authority’s rule is to reheat leftovers until steaming hot — 60°C or above all the way through, not warmed at the edges. That is what makes the three-day window a safe guideline rather than a hopeful one, and [the leftovers post](/resources/how-long-do-leftovers-keep) has the rest of the timing, including the two-hour/four-hour rule that starts the clock before the container reaches the fridge.

## When tonight is not happening

Some nights there is no path to cooking, and the freezer is the answer rather than a fourth day in the fridge. Portion what will not be eaten inside the window and [freeze it while it is still inside it](/resources/freezer-times-explained). A frozen container of Sunday’s dinner is a weeknight saved later; a container that slid past Wednesday is nothing.

## Why leftovers catch people out

A container of leftovers is the one item in the fridge with no label, no date and no plan. Cooked once, meant to be eaten, then not — simply because three days closed before anyone circled back, which is how leftovers end up as one of the biggest quiet contributors to the [$2,500 a year](/resources/household-food-waste-by-the-numbers) an average household bins.

Pantry treats the container the way it treats everything off the receipt: logged with a date and a countdown the moment it goes in. And when it reaches its last day, the [dinner suggestion](/resources/receipt-to-meals) starts from it rather than from a recipe search, built only from what is already in the house, so “what is for dinner” is answered by the thing that needs eating first. The five dinners above are that logic done by hand. [The full shelf-life reference](/resources/how-long-food-lasts) has the numbers for everything else running on a short clock.
