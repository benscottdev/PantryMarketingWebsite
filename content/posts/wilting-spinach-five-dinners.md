Spinach has one of the shortest windows of anything in a typical fridge: five to seven days, per [Sustainability Victoria's storage guidance](https://www.sustainability.vic.gov.au/recycling-and-reducing-waste-at-home/avoid-waste/food-waste/guide-for-storing-fresh-produce). It goes from crisp to sad well before it becomes unsafe. That gap — still fine to eat, no longer appealing raw — is exactly where a bag of spinach quietly turns from dinner into a bin decision.

The good news: wilted spinach is a cooking problem, not a food-safety one. Heat fixes texture. Here are five ways to use it before it goes soft enough that even cooking will not save it.

## 1. Wilted into pasta, right at the end

Spinach past its raw-salad best is perfect for this, because cooking is what you were going to do to it anyway. Stir a few handfuls in during the last minute, off the heat, in the residual warmth of the sauce. It collapses to almost nothing — a going-soft bag disappears into the meal without announcing itself.

## 2. Into an omelette or frittata

Chop it roughly and cook it down in the pan first — a soft bag of spinach reduces to a tablespoon of cooked greens almost instantly. Fold it through eggs with whatever cheese is in the fridge. This is a proper weeknight dinner, not a way to hide vegetables in something else: twelve minutes, one pan.

## 3. Blitzed into a soup

Any soup with a stock base — pumpkin, potato and leek, even a tin of tomatoes — takes a big handful of spinach in the last five minutes and blends it in without changing the flavour much. This is the move for spinach that has gone past slightly soft into limp: texture stops mattering once it is blended.

## 4. A quick curry or dal base

Spinach wilted into a simple dal or curry (lentils, tinned tomato, whatever spices are on hand) adds bulk and nutrition without adding a shopping trip. It also freezes well afterward, which a raw bag of spinach does not — so this is the move when there is more spinach than one dinner will use.

## 5. Sautéed as a side, with garlic

The simplest option: a hot pan, a little oil, garlic, and the spinach until it collapses — two or three minutes. This is the fastest of the five, and it works alongside almost anything already planned for dinner, so it does not need its own meal slot.

## How to wilt spinach on purpose

If you landed here wanting the technique rather than the rescue mission: wilting spinach is the fastest job in cooking. A hot pan with a little oil, the leaves in, and thirty seconds to two minutes of tossing — it collapses the moment it hits real heat. Off the stove, the residual warmth of a just-drained pot of pasta or a finished soup does the same thing with no pan at all. There is no timing to get right. Spinach is done wilting when it looks done, and it cannot really be overdone short of leaving it to fry.

The only note worth adding: a full bag cooks down to a fraction of its size, so add more than looks reasonable. That shrinkage is exactly why the five dinners above absorb a whole going-soft bag without anyone noticing.

## The pattern across all five

Every one of these treats wilting as a texture change, not a spoilage signal. If the spinach smells fine and is not slimy, cooking it solves the problem. The only real failure mode is leaving it long enough to cross from wilted into spoiled — which, per the five-to-seven-day window, tends to happen fast once it starts.
